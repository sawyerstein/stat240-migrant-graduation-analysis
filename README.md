# Migrant vs. Non-Migrant Student Graduation Rates

A statistical analysis examining differences in six-year high school graduation rates between migrant and non-migrant students using data from the New York State Education Department (NYSED).

## Project Overview

This STAT 240 group project investigated whether there was a statistically significant difference in graduation rates between migrant and non-migrant students in New York.

Using NYSED graduation data, we compared six-year graduation outcomes and applied statistical inference to evaluate whether the observed difference between the two populations was statistically significant.

## Key Findings

- Migrant students had an observed graduation rate of approximately 64%, compared with 89% for non-migrant students.
- A hypothesis test for the difference in proportions produced a p-value of approximately 4.36e-16.
- The results provided strong statistical evidence of a difference in graduation rates between migrant and non-migrant students in the analyzed population.
- The analysis also identified limitations related to statistical assumptions and the ability to generalize the results beyond the population studied.

## Methods

- Data cleaning and preparation in R
- Exploratory data visualization
- Comparison of population proportions
- Binomial modeling
- Hypothesis testing
- Statistical interpretation and discussion of assumptions

## Data Source

Data was obtained from the New York State Education Department (NYSED) Graduation Rate Database.
